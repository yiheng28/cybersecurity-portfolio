## Learning Journey

My cybersecurity learning journey is an ongoing process of developing my theoretical knowledge and practical technical skills. Although my previous academic background focused on Computer Science and Cloud Computing, I am now expanding my knowledge into cybersecurity through my Master of Cybersecurity programme at Universiti Malaya, which I started in September 2026.

Throughout my studies, I have been introduced to different cybersecurity topics through courses such as Cyber Security and Advanced Computer Penetration and Defence. These courses have helped me understand cybersecurity from both theoretical and practical perspectives.

One interesting experience from my Cyber Security course is the attendance activity. Instead of simply providing an attendance password, the lecturer gives students different tasks and challenges that require us to discover the password independently. This encourages me to think critically, investigate the given tasks, and apply my problem-solving skills rather than simply following instructions. It has also helped me understand the importance of curiosity and independent learning in cybersecurity.

For Advanced Computer Penetration and Defence, the lecturer provides practical laboratory exercises and tutorials to help us strengthen our technical knowledge. These activities allow me to practise using cybersecurity tools and understand how different penetration-testing techniques work. Both courses involve some similar tools and practical activities, which gives me more opportunities to become familiar with the tools through repeated practice.

Outside the classroom, I have also completed two practical projects. My first project involved setting up an isolated virtual cybersecurity laboratory using Kali Linux and Metasploitable 2 through UTM on macOS. During the setup process, I encountered difficulties configuring the network connection between the two virtual machines. Initially, they could not communicate with each other. I investigated the virtual network settings and discovered that the machines needed to use the same network environment. After configuring both machines to use Shared Network, I successfully established communication between them. This experience taught me the importance of understanding virtual networking and troubleshooting technical problems.

My second project focused on network scanning and service detection using Nmap. I performed basic scans and used the `-sV` option to identify services and their versions running on the target machine. Through this exercise, I learned how to identify open ports, interpret scanning results, and understand the potential security risks associated with exposed network services.

Although I have gained some practical experience, I still find it challenging to interpret network scanning results and understand the security implications of different services. I can identify open ports and service versions using Nmap, but I need more practice understanding how these services operate and how potential weaknesses can affect a system. I plan to improve this knowledge through additional laboratory exercises and independent research.

Moving forward, my improvement goal is to complete all the practical laboratories, tutorials, and learning activities assigned by my lecturers by 31 January 2027. I will measure my progress by tracking the number of assigned activities completed, ensuring that I can successfully perform the required tasks, and documenting my learning outcomes with screenshots and explanations. I also plan to continue my Cisco Networking Academy and TryHackMe learning activities to gain additional practical experience.

Overall, these learning experiences have helped me understand that cybersecurity requires continuous learning, independent problem-solving, and practical experience. I look forward to exploring more cybersecurity areas throughout my Master's programme before deciding on my future career specialisation.
